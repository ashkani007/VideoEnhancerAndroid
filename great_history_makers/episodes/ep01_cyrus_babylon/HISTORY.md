# Episode 1 — Historical basis and claim check

**Subject:** the Persian takeover of Babylon, autumn 539 BCE.
**Rule:** every line of narration must fall under *established* or be explicitly framed as a *claim*.

## 1. Established (contemporary Babylonian documents)

| Point | Source |
|---|---|
| Cyrus's army fought the Babylonian army at **Opis** on the Tigris and won (late Sept/early Oct 539 BCE). | Nabonidus Chronicle (ABC 7, BM 35382), col. iii |
| **Sippar** was taken "without battle" (14 Tashritu ≈ 10 Oct 539). | Nabonidus Chronicle |
| **Ugbaru** (Gubaru), governor of Gutium, and Cyrus's army **entered Babylon without battle** (16 Tashritu ≈ 12 Oct 539). Nabonidus was captured. | Nabonidus Chronicle |
| **Cyrus himself entered Babylon** on 3 Arahsamnu (≈ 29 Oct 539). "Green twigs were spread in front of him"; peace was imposed on the city. | Nabonidus Chronicle |
| Rites at **Esagila** and other temples continued without interruption. | Nabonidus Chronicle |
| Administrative continuity: Babylonian officials and the temple economy kept running under Persian rule. | Babylonian economic/administrative tablets (see Briant; Kuhrt) |

Calendar conversions follow standard tables (Parker & Dubberstein); exact Julian days vary by ±1 between authors.

## 2. Royal propaganda (presents the conqueror's own message)

- **Cyrus Cylinder** (BM 90920), written in Akkadian after the conquest: Marduk, angry with Nabonidus, looks for a righteous king, chooses Cyrus, and lets him enter "without battle or fighting". It says Cyrus restored sanctuaries and sent gods and peoples back to their homes. This is a traditional **Mesopotamian royal building inscription**: a statement of legitimacy, not neutral reporting.
- **Verse Account of Nabonidus:** a pro-Persian text attacking Nabonidus's religious policy.

→ Narration line 3 ("Cyrus **proclaimed** that Babylon's own god had chosen him") reports the Cylinder's *claim* as a claim.

## 3. Later or disputed traditions (not used as fact)

- **Herodotus 1.188–191:** the Persians diverted the Euphrates and entered along the riverbed while the people celebrated a festival. This Greek account comes about a century later and conflicts with the Babylonian record of an entry without battle. It is disputed.
- **Xenophon, Cyropaedia 7.5:** a similar story, but the *Cyropaedia* is an idealised, partly fictional work.
- **Daniel 5 (Belshazzar's feast, the writing on the wall):** a theological/literary text. Belshazzar was Nabonidus's son and regent, not king.
- **Ezra 1:1–4 (edict permitting the Judeans to return):** scholars debate how authentic the wording is. The Cylinder does not mention Judeans.
- **"First charter of human rights":** a modern 20th-century interpretation. It is not supported by the text or by mainstream scholarship. The Cylinder does not abolish slavery or proclaim universal rights. **Not used.**
- **"Liberated every inhabitant":** not supported. Many Babylonians may have accepted the change of ruler, but the sources are mainly propaganda from the winning side. **Not used.**

## 4. Artistic reconstruction (visual choices, flagged on screen)

- **Mosaic style:** Roman/Hellenistic tessellated mosaic is used purely as the **artistic idiom**. No such mosaic of these events exists.
- **Cyrus's appearance:** no contemporary portrait survives. Crown, purple robe and staff are a reconstruction based on later Achaemenid royal imagery (Persepolis, c. 500 BCE) and Xenophon's description of royal purple.
- **Soldiers:** Median riding dress (hood, tunic, trousers) and Persian robes come from Persepolis/Susa reliefs, which are a few decades later. The golden-eagle standard follows Xenophon (*Cyropaedia* 7.1.4; *Anabasis* 1.10.12). These are Greek sources.
- **Ishtar Gate:** built under Nebuchadnezzar II (c. 575 BCE), so it stood in 539. The animals are simplified from its aurochs and mušḫuššu reliefs. The Etemenanki ziggurat and Processional Way lions are attested.
- **Scene 3 meeting:** shows a plausible encounter with officials. The gestures are generic, and **no speech or ceremony is presented as fact**. The green branches on the ground follow the Chronicle. An on-screen note says "Artistic reconstruction".
- **Music:** a modern evocative score. Music of the period cannot be reconstructed.

## 5. Narration claim check

| # | Line | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | In 539 BCE, Cyrus the Great of Persia set his sights on Babylon. | Established (campaign of 539) |
| 2 | A Babylonian chronicle records that his army entered the city without a battle. | Established, and attributed to its source (the Opis battle happened before, outside the city) |
| 3 | Temple rites went on, and Cyrus proclaimed that Babylon's own god had chosen him. | Rites: Chronicle. Proclamation: framed as Cylinder propaganda |
| 4 | One of the greatest cities of the ancient world now had a new ruler. | Established ("one of the greatest" is a fair, hedged judgement) |
