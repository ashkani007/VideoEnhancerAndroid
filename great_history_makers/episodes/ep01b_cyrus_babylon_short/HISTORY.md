# Episode 1b: historical basis and claim check

The narration is the client's ElevenLabs voice-over. It is used unchanged; its wording was transcribed and verified by forced alignment.

| Narration | Status | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| "In 539 BCE, Cyrus the Great turned his attention to Babylon" | Established | Nabonidus Chronicle (ABC 7) |
| "one of the ancient world's greatest cities" | Fair judgement | Excavated size of Babylon; Herodotus 1.178 (later, exaggerated) |
| "After the Persian victory at Opis, Babylon came under Persian control" | Established | Nabonidus Chronicle: battle at Opis on the Tigris, then Sippar and Babylon taken |
| "Ancient accounts describe the city's capture with little resistance, though details differ" | Established | Chronicle and Cyrus Cylinder: entry "without battle". Herodotus 1.191 and Xenophon describe a river stratagem; the accounts conflict |
| "Cyrus entered Babylon and presented himself as a legitimate king, honoring local traditions and the god Marduk" | Established as self-presentation | Chronicle (Cyrus's entry on 3 Arahsamnu; temple rites continued); Cylinder (Marduk's choice) |
| "The famous Cyrus Cylinder records his royal claims and his restoration of religious sites and displaced communities" | Established (content of the text) | Cyrus Cylinder, BM 90920, ll. 30–34 |
| "It is an extraordinary artifact, but not a modern declaration of human rights" | Scholarly consensus | Finkel (ed.) 2013; Kuhrt 2007; van der Spek 2014 |
| "Babylon became part of a vast Persian Empire" | Established | Briant 2002 |

## Visual decisions (artistic reconstruction)

- **Opis (shot 4):** the battle is shown as a stylised frieze with no casualties. The Chronicle confirms a Persian victory and a Babylonian retreat; it does not record any tactical detail.
- **Entry (shot 8):** Persian troops pass through the Ishtar Gate. **Cyrus is not shown at the gate**, because the sources do not say which gate was used. The Chronicle says Ugbaru's troops entered first and Cyrus arrived about two weeks later.
- **Marduk (shot 11):** the god's emblems (the *marru* spade and the *mušḫuššu* dragon) are shown instead of a cult statue, whose appearance is unknown.
- **Restoration (shot 13):** this illustrates what the Cylinder *claims* (rebuilding sanctuaries, people returning). The narration attributes it to the Cylinder.
- **Map (shot 16):** simplified and approximate extent c. 539 BCE. Egypt (conquered 525 BCE) is deliberately excluded.
- **Portrait (shot 18):** an imagined likeness. No contemporary portrait of Cyrus survives.
- **Architecture:** the Ishtar Gate, Etemenanki and the Processional Way date from Nebuchadnezzar II (c. 600–562 BCE), so they stood in 539. Their shapes are simplified.
- **Costumes:** Persian dress follows later Achaemenid reliefs (Persepolis, Susa), which date to a few decades after 539.
- **Style:** Roman mosaic is the chosen artistic idiom. It is not a claim about Achaemenid or Babylonian art.
