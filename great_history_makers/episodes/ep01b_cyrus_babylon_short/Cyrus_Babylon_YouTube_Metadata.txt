DESCRIPTION
In 539 BCE, Cyrus the Great of Persia turned his attention to Babylon, one of the greatest cities of the ancient world. After the Persian victory at Opis on the Tigris, Babylon came under Persian control. Babylonian records describe the city's capture with little resistance; later Greek writers tell a different story.

Cyrus presented himself as Babylon's legitimate king, chosen by the god Marduk. The famous Cyrus Cylinder (British Museum) records these royal claims and his restoration of sanctuaries and displaced communities. It is an extraordinary artifact, but it is a royal inscription in the Mesopotamian tradition, not a modern declaration of human rights.

Sources: the Nabonidus Chronicle; the Cyrus Cylinder (BM 90920); Herodotus 1.188–191; I. Finkel (ed.), The Cyrus Cylinder (2013); A. Kuhrt, The Persian Empire (2007); P. Briant, From Cyrus to Alexander (2002).

Artistic note: this animation is an artistic reconstruction in the style of ancient Roman mosaics. Roman mosaic is our visual idiom, not a claim about the art of Achaemenid Persia or Babylon. Faces, costumes, scenes and the map are interpretive and simplified. The battle and the meeting scenes are illustrative.
