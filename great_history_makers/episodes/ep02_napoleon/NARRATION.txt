He rose from an artillery officer to become the most feared ruler in Europe.
Napoleon Bonaparte crowned himself Emperor of the French in 1804.
His armies won extraordinary victories, and his empire reshaped the continent.
But Napoleon wanted more.
In 1812, he invaded Russia with a vast multinational army.
He reached Moscow, only to find much of the city burning.
Then came the retreat.
Hunger, disease, exhaustion, and bitter cold devastated his forces.
His enemies united.
In 1814, Napoleon abdicated and was exiled to Elba.
He returned the following year for one final gamble.
At Waterloo, his army was defeated.
This time, exile meant a remote island in the Atlantic: Saint Helena.
Napoleon died there in 1821.
He conquered kingdoms, changed Europe, and became a legend.
But he could not conquer the consequences of his own ambition.
