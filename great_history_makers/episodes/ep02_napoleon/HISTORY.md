# Episode 02: Napoleon, from Emperor to Exile (historical basis and claim check)

The narration is client-approved and used verbatim. Each line is checked below.

## 1. Established facts

| Line | Status | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| "He rose from an artillery officer …" | Established | Commissioned second lieutenant of artillery in 1785 (La Fère regiment); made his name commanding the artillery at the Siege of Toulon (1793) |
| "… the most feared ruler in Europe" | Fair judgement (rhetorical) | The coalitions of 1798–1815 were formed against him |
| "crowned himself Emperor of the French in 1804" | Established | 2 December 1804, Notre-Dame. Pius VII anointed him; Napoleon placed the crown on his own head (he had been proclaimed Emperor in May 1804) |
| "His armies won extraordinary victories … reshaped the continent" | Established | Austerlitz 1805, Jena 1806, Friedland 1807, Wagram 1809; end of the Holy Roman Empire (1806); Confederation of the Rhine; Duchy of Warsaw; Napoleonic Code |
| "In 1812, he invaded Russia with a vast multinational army" | Established | Crossed the Niemen on 24 June 1812; often estimated at 450,000–600,000 men, with roughly half from non-French contingents |
| "He reached Moscow, only to find much of the city burning" | Established | Entered on 14 September 1812. The fires (15–18 September), widely attributed to Russian arson under Governor Rostopchin, destroyed most of the city |
| "Then came the retreat … hunger, disease, exhaustion, bitter cold" | Established, not monocausal | Retreat from 19 October. Heavy losses before the severe cold from typhus and dysentery, starvation, desertion, Cossack harassment and battles (Vyazma, Krasnoi, Berezina). The script does not blame winter alone, and the on-screen note dates the retreat October–December |
| "His enemies united" | Established | Sixth Coalition, 1813: Russia, Prussia, Austria, Britain, Sweden and others; Leipzig, October 1813 |
| "In 1814, Napoleon abdicated and was exiled to Elba" | Established | Abdication 6 April 1814; Treaty of Fontainebleau; farewell to the Guard on 20 April; arrived at Elba 4 May |
| "He returned the following year …" | Established | Landed at Golfe-Juan on 1 March 1815; the Hundred Days |
| "At Waterloo, his army was defeated" | Established | 18 June 1815, against Wellington's Allied army and Blücher's Prussians |
| "… a remote island in the Atlantic: Saint Helena" | Established | South Atlantic; arrived October 1815 aboard HMS Northumberland; lived at Longwood |
| "Napoleon died there in 1821" | Established | 5 May 1821 |
| Last two lines | Interpretive conclusion | Standard historiographical judgement; phrased as reflection, not fact |

## 2. Artistic reconstruction (shown, not claimed)

- **Toulon (shot 1):** the battery, gunners and pointing gesture are illustrative.
- **Coronation (shot 3):** a simplified nave. The moment of lifting the crown follows the accepted account; the hall layout is not accurate.
- **Map (shots 4 and 10):** hand-simplified coastlines; the borders c. 1811 are approximate.
  - Spain was contested, not secure.
  - Labels mark the main victories.
  - The coalition arrows are symbolic.
- **Napoleon over the map (shot 5):** labelled "Artistic reconstruction".
- **Poklonnaya Hill (shot 7):** Napoleon waited there on 14 September for a delegation that never came. The skyline shows pre-1812 buildings only: Kremlin towers, Ivan the Great Bell Tower, the cathedrals and St Basil's. The Cathedral of Christ the Saviour (begun 1839) is deliberately excluded.
- **Fontainebleau (shot 11):** the horseshoe staircase and the Old Guard follow the "Adieux" scene, simplified.
- **Golfe-Juan (shot 12):** the brig *Inconstant* is in the background; the escort is simplified.
- **Waterloo (shot 13):** symbolic composition (column attack, Allied volleys, cuirassiers). It is not a tactical map, and there is no gore.
- **Arc de Triomphe (shot 16):** commissioned in 1806, completed in 1836, after Napoleon's death; labelled as a legacy image.
- **Bicorne on the rock (shot 17):** symbolic.

## 3. Uniforms and objects

- **French line infantry 1804–12:** blue habit with white lapels and turnbacks, red collar and cuffs, white breeches, black gaiters, shako with brass plate, calfskin knapsack, Charleville-pattern musket with bayonet.
- **Colours:**
  - **1804 pattern:** white lozenge, blue and red corners, eagle finial. Used before 1812.
  - **Vertical tricolour:** used for 1815 scenes.
- **Old Guard:** bearskin with red plume and epaulettes. **British infantry 1815:** red coat, "Belgic" shako, grey trousers. **Cuirassiers:** steel cuirass, crested helmet.
- **Napoleon:**
  - 1793: artillery officer's blue coat, long hair.
  - 1804: crimson velvet mantle sewn with golden bees, ermine, gold laurel wreath.
  - 1812–15: grey redingote over the green chasseur coat, bicorne worn en bataille.
  - 1815–21: green coat, white waistcoat and breeches, heavier build.
- **Style:** the ancient-mosaic look is a modern artistic treatment, not a claim that these events were depicted in mosaics.
