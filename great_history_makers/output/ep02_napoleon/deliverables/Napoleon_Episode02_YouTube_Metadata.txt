DESCRIPTION
He rose from an artillery officer to Emperor of the French. In 1804 Napoleon crowned himself at Notre-Dame, and his victories reshaped Europe. In 1812 he invaded Russia with a vast multinational army, reached a burning Moscow, and lost most of his army in the retreat to hunger, disease, exhaustion and cold, as well as to battles and Cossack attacks. His enemies united; he abdicated in 1814 and was exiled to Elba, returned in 1815, was defeated at Waterloo, and died on Saint Helena in 1821.

Sources: D. Chandler, The Campaigns of Napoleon; A. Zamoyski, 1812: Napoleon's Fatal March on Moscow; D. Lieven, Russia Against Napoleon; A. Roberts, Napoleon the Great; Fondation Napoléon (napoleon.org).

Artistic note: an artistic reconstruction in the style of ancient mosaics. The mosaic style is a modern creative treatment, not a claim that these events were recorded in mosaics. Scenes, faces, maps and uniforms are simplified and interpretive. The Arc de Triomphe (completed 1836) appears as a symbol of legacy.
