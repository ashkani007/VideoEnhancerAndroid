In 539 BCE, Cyrus the Great turned his attention to Babylon, one of the ancient world's greatest cities.
After the Persian victory at Opis, Babylon came under Persian control.
Ancient accounts describe the city's capture with little resistance, though details differ.
Cyrus entered Babylon and presented himself as a legitimate king, honoring local traditions and the god Marduk.
The famous Cyrus Cylinder records his royal claims and his restoration of religious sites and displaced communities.
It is an extraordinary artifact, but not a modern declaration of human rights.
Babylon became part of a vast Persian Empire.
More than twenty-five centuries later, the story of Cyrus still raises questions about power, conquest, and how rulers wish to be remembered.
