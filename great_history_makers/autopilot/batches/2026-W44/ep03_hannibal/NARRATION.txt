In 218 BCE, a Carthaginian general did what Rome thought impossible.
Hannibal marched his army from Spain, over the Pyrenees, across the Rhône, and into the Alps.
He brought war elephants.
Snow, rockfalls and hostile mountain tribes cost him thousands of men.
Yet he reached Italy.
At the Trebia, at Lake Trasimene, and at Cannae, he destroyed Roman armies.
Cannae became one of the worst defeats in Roman history.
But Rome refused to surrender.
For fifteen years, Hannibal fought in Italy and never took the city.
Called home to defend Carthage, he was defeated at Zama in 202 BCE.
He won the battles.
Rome won the war.
