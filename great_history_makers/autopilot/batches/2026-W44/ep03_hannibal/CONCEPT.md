# Hannibal: Across The Alps (Episode 03)

**Period:** 218–202 BCE  ·  **Status:** script pending client approval

**Hook:** A general marches war elephants over the Alps to attack Rome from behind.

## Narration (send to ElevenLabs unchanged once approved)

1. In 218 BCE, a Carthaginian general did what Rome thought impossible.
2. Hannibal marched his army from Spain, over the Pyrenees, across the Rhône, and into the Alps.
3. He brought war elephants.
4. Snow, rockfalls and hostile mountain tribes cost him thousands of men.
5. Yet he reached Italy.
6. At the Trebia, at Lake Trasimene, and at Cannae, he destroyed Roman armies.
7. Cannae became one of the worst defeats in Roman history.
8. But Rome refused to surrender.
9. For fifteen years, Hannibal fought in Italy and never took the city.
10. Called home to defend Carthage, he was defeated at Zama in 202 BCE.
11. He won the battles.
12. Rome won the war.

## History check

**Established**

- Hannibal left Iberia in 218 BCE, crossed the Pyrenees, the Rhône and the Alps, and entered northern Italy (Polybius 3; Livy 21).
- He brought elephants (Polybius gives 37 at the Rhône); most died during the first winter.
- Victories at the Trebia (218), Lake Trasimene (217) and Cannae (216 BCE); Cannae is among Rome's worst defeats.
- He campaigned in Italy until 203 BCE without besieging Rome; recalled to Africa; defeated by Scipio at Zama, 202 BCE.

**Propaganda**

- Roman authors (Livy) present Hannibal as cruel and treacherous; we avoid character claims.

**Disputed**

- The exact Alpine pass (Col de la Traversette, Mont Cenis, Little St Bernard and others).
- Losses during the crossing: Polybius says he arrived in Italy with 20,000 infantry and 6,000 cavalry; the starting strength and the share lost in the Alps are uncertain. The script says only 'thousands'.

**Reconstruction**

- Faces, armour details, elephant tack and battle choreography are artistic reconstructions; label 'Artistic reconstruction'.
