In 1588, King Philip the Second of Spain sent a great fleet against England.
His plan was to carry an invasion army from Flanders across the Channel.
About a hundred and thirty ships sailed up the English coast in a vast crescent.
Off Calais, the English sent burning fireships into the anchored fleet at night.
The Spanish ships cut their cables and scattered.
At Gravelines, English guns battered them at close range.
Then the wind turned against Spain.
Unable to return through the Channel, the Armada sailed around Scotland and Ireland.
Storms wrecked dozens of ships, and thousands of men never came home.
Queen Elizabeth the First had survived the greatest threat of her reign.
England called it a victory sent by God.
