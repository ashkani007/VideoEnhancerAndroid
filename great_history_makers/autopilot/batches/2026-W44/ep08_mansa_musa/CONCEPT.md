# Mansa Musa: The Golden Pilgrimage (Episode 08)

**Period:** 1324–1325  ·  **Status:** script pending client approval

**Hook:** A West African king's pilgrimage is so rich that it shakes the price of gold in Cairo.

## Narration (send to ElevenLabs unchanged once approved)

1. In 1324, a king from West Africa set out for Mecca.
2. Mansa Musa ruled the Mali Empire, rich in gold from the lands of the upper Niger and Senegal rivers.
3. His caravan crossed the Sahara with thousands of followers, and camels loaded with gold.
4. In Cairo, he gave away so much that, according to the scholar al-Umari, the value of gold there fell for years.
5. Musa completed the pilgrimage and returned with scholars and an architect.
6. In Timbuktu, the great mosque of Djinguereber rose.
7. The city grew into a centre of trade and Islamic learning.
8. Half a century later, a European map showed him on a throne, holding a golden nugget.
9. Europe was beginning to discover Mali.
10. Mali had long known the world.

## History check

**Established**

- Mansa Musa ruled Mali c. 1312–1337; he made the hajj in 1324–1325 via Cairo.
- Al-Umari (writing c. 1337–1338, from Cairo informants) reports that his gifts depressed the value of gold in Cairo for years.
- He brought back scholars and the Andalusi poet-architect Abu Ishaq al-Sahili; the Djinguereber mosque is traditionally dated to 1327.
- The Catalan Atlas (1375) shows him enthroned holding a gold nugget.

**Propaganda**

- Arabic accounts emphasise his piety and wealth; numbers (60,000 followers, 80–100 camels of gold) are hearsay — the script says only 'thousands'.

**Disputed**

- Size of the caravan and quantity of gold; extent of the Cairo price effect; modern 'richest man ever' claims (not used).

**Reconstruction**

- No contemporary portrait; the king is stylised; Mecca is shown only symbolically.
