In 1324, a king from West Africa set out for Mecca.
Mansa Musa ruled the Mali Empire, rich in gold from the lands of the upper Niger and Senegal rivers.
His caravan crossed the Sahara with thousands of followers, and camels loaded with gold.
In Cairo, he gave away so much that, according to the scholar al-Umari, the value of gold there fell for years.
Musa completed the pilgrimage and returned with scholars and an architect.
In Timbuktu, the great mosque of Djinguereber rose.
The city grew into a centre of trade and Islamic learning.
Half a century later, a European map showed him on a throne, holding a golden nugget.
Europe was beginning to discover Mali.
Mali had long known the world.
