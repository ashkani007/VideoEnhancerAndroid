# Ashoka: The Conqueror Who Repented (Episode 07)

**Period:** r. c. 268–232 BCE  ·  **Status:** script pending client approval

**Hook:** An emperor writes his own war crimes into stone and promises to change.

## Narration (send to ElevenLabs unchanged once approved)

1. Around 261 BCE, the Mauryan emperor Ashoka conquered the kingdom of Kalinga.
2. His own inscriptions record the cost: a hundred and fifty thousand people deported, a hundred thousand killed, and many more dead.
3. Then he did something few conquerors ever did.
4. He publicly expressed remorse.
5. Ashoka embraced the teaching of the Buddha, and a policy he called Dhamma.
6. He had his messages carved on rocks and pillars across his empire.
7. They urged respect for all religions, kindness to servants and animals, and restraint.
8. He kept his army, and he kept his power.
9. But he tried to rule by persuasion.
10. His script was forgotten for centuries, until it was deciphered in 1837.
11. Today, his lion capital is the national emblem of India.

## History check

**Established**

- Ashoka ruled the Mauryan Empire c. 268–232 BCE; Kalinga conquered c. 261 BCE.
- Rock Edict XIII: 150,000 deported, 100,000 killed, and many times that number perished; it expresses remorse.
- Edicts on rocks and pillars in Prakrit (Brahmi and Kharosthi scripts), with Greek and Aramaic versions in the northwest; they promote dhamma, tolerance of sects, care for people and animals.
- James Prinsep deciphered Brahmi in 1837; the Lion Capital of Sarnath is India's State Emblem.

**Propaganda**

- The edicts are Ashoka's own self-presentation; later Buddhist legends (Ashokavadana) exaggerate his early cruelty.

**Disputed**

- Whether his conversion followed or preceded Kalinga; how far policy changed in practice (the edicts keep the army and capital punishment).

**Reconstruction**

- No portrait exists; Ashoka is stylised; edict illustrations are interpretive.
