Around 261 BCE, the Mauryan emperor Ashoka conquered the kingdom of Kalinga.
His own inscriptions record the cost: a hundred and fifty thousand people deported, a hundred thousand killed, and many more dead.
Then he did something few conquerors ever did.
He publicly expressed remorse.
Ashoka embraced the teaching of the Buddha, and a policy he called Dhamma.
He had his messages carved on rocks and pillars across his empire.
They urged respect for all religions, kindness to servants and animals, and restraint.
He kept his army, and he kept his power.
But he tried to rule by persuasion.
His script was forgotten for centuries, until it was deciphered in 1837.
Today, his lion capital is the national emblem of India.
