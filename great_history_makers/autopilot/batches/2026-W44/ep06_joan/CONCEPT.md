# Joan Of Arc: The Maid Of Orléans (Episode 06)

**Period:** c. 1412–1431  ·  **Status:** script pending client approval

**Hook:** A teenage peasant girl changes the course of the Hundred Years' War.

## Narration (send to ElevenLabs unchanged once approved)

1. She was a peasant girl from a village in eastern France.
2. Around the age of thirteen, Joan said she began to hear the voices of saints.
3. They told her to help drive the English out of France.
4. In 1429, at about seventeen, she reached the court of the uncrowned Charles the Seventh.
5. He gave her armor, a banner, and a place with his army.
6. At Orléans, the English siege was broken in nine days.
7. Two months later, Charles was crowned king at Reims.
8. In 1430, Joan was captured and handed over to the English.
9. A church court tried her for heresy.
10. On the thirtieth of May 1431, she was burned at the stake in Rouen.
11. She was about nineteen.
12. Twenty-five years later, a new trial overturned her conviction.

## History check

**Established**

- Born c. 1412 at Domrémy; she testified her voices began at about age 13 (trial record).
- Reached Chinon in early 1429; the siege of Orléans was lifted 8 May 1429, nine days after her arrival.
- Charles VII crowned at Reims on 17 July 1429, with Joan present.
- Captured at Compiègne in May 1430 by Burgundians and handed to the English; tried by a church court under Bishop Cauchon; burned in Rouen on 30 May 1431.
- The nullification trial in 1456 overturned the verdict; canonised in 1920.

**Propaganda**

- The 1431 trial record was shaped by a hostile court; the 1456 retrial testimony was shaped by the opposite interest.

**Disputed**

- Her exact birth date; the nature of her voices (religious, medical and political readings).

**Reconstruction**

- Faces and the saints are stylised; saints shown as light silhouettes and labelled as her testimony.
