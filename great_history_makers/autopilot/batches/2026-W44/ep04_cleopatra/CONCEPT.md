**Hook:** The last ruler of independent Egypt plays Rome's strongmen against each other.

## Narration (send to ElevenLabs unchanged once approved)

1. She was the last pharaoh of an independent Egypt, and her family was not Egyptian.
2. Cleopatra the Seventh belonged to a Macedonian Greek dynasty that had ruled Egypt for more than two centuries.
3. Ancient writers say she spoke many languages, and was the first of her line to learn Egyptian.
4. To keep her kingdom alive, she allied with the most powerful men in Rome.
5. First Julius Caesar, who helped her win the throne.
6. Then Mark Antony.
7. In 31 BCE, their fleet was defeated by Octavian at Actium.
8. A year later, as Octavian's army entered Alexandria, Cleopatra died.
9. Ancient accounts blame a snake's bite. The truth is uncertain.
10. Egypt became a Roman province.
11. And her story was written by her enemies.

## History check

**Established**

- Cleopatra VII (69–30 BCE) of the Macedonian Greek Ptolemaic dynasty (ruling since 305 BCE).
- Plutarch (Antony 27) says she spoke many languages and was the first Ptolemy to learn Egyptian.
- Caesar backed her claim in the Alexandrian War (48–47 BCE); her alliance with Antony began in 41 BCE.
- Octavian defeated Antony and Cleopatra at Actium in 31 BCE; she died in August 30 BCE; Egypt became a Roman province.

**Propaganda**

- Octavian's propaganda and later Roman writers portray her as a seductress; the script's last line flags this.

**Disputed**

- Cause of death: asp bite (ancient tradition) vs poison; Plutarch himself says nobody knows the truth.
- Her son Caesarion was nominally co-ruler; 'last pharaoh of an independent Egypt' refers to her as last active ruler.

**Reconstruction**

- Her appearance is unknown beyond coins and contested statues; the portrait is stylised.
