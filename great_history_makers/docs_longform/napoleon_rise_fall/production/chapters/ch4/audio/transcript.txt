As First Consul, Bonaparte rebuilt the French state. He made peace with the Catholic Church. He founded the Bank of France and the Legion of Honour. He sent prefects to govern every department from Paris. And in 1804 he gave France the Civil Code, a single body of law for the whole country, which would outlive him by centuries.
That same year he took the final step. On the second of December, 1804, in the cathedral of Notre-Dame, in the presence of the Pope, he placed the crown on his own head, and became Emperor of the French.
Britain had been at war with France again since 1803. In 1805, Austria and Russia joined a new coalition against him.
Napoleon turned the army he had gathered on the Channel coast around, and marched it to the Rhine. Seven army corps swung across southern Germany so quickly that an Austrian army under General Mack was cut off and surrounded at Ulm. On the twentieth of October, Mack surrendered. In November, the French entered Vienna.
At sea, the news was very different. A day after Ulm, Nelson destroyed the French and Spanish fleets at Trafalgar. Britain would rule the oceans for the rest of the war.
On land, the decisive moment came near the Moravian village of Austerlitz, on the second of December 1805, a year to the day after the coronation.
Outnumbered, Napoleon deliberately weakened his right wing, to tempt the Russian and Austrian armies into attacking it. They took the bait. As the sun burned through the morning mist, Marshal Soult's divisions climbed the Pratzen Heights at the centre of the allied line, and split their army in two.
By nightfall the coalition was broken. Austria made peace within weeks. Austerlitz became the model of a Napoleonic victory, studied by soldiers for the next hundred years.
